# SPR256 — `.256` sprite

Claims about the `.256` sprite payload: its container, trailer, palette and
run-length grammar; the shipped `CSprite256` loader and byte decoders; and how
the cursor, unit, structure, equipment and backpack consumers address its
frames. One claim covers the `graphics\infowindow` BMP pictures that share the
equipment portrait geometry. Spec:
[`formats/spr256/format.md`](../formats/spr256/format.md). Format of this file:
[registry.md](registry.md).

## Terms

- Bucket-B: the 8 `.256` files that hold one frame followed by an appended
  section (`SPR256-EXC-005`, `SPR256-EXC-017`).
- No-palette arrows: the 6 projectile-arrow `.256` files that carry no embedded
  palette (`SPR256-VAR-004`).
- A short ID such as `COUNT-002` names the claim `SPR256-COUNT-002` of this
  ledger.

### SPR256-CURSOR-046

- Files: `cursors/smove.256`, `sattack.256`, `sdefend.256`, `spatrol.256`,
  `scast.256`.
- Every payload has a palette and walks whole to trailer count 1.
- `FUN_0046d3f0` registers each at hotspot `(0,0)` with the same large period

- G2: pixels, geometry and count are resource bytes; registration order and
  hotspot are executable constants.

**Confidence.** High. Five complete file walks on two roots are joined to five
constructor calls and to the independently identified minimap consumer.

## Container, trailer and corpus

|---|---|---|---|---|

| SPR256-COUNT-002 | The frame count equals the number of frame records before the 4-byte trailer; `u32@0` is palette entry 0, not a count. | High | ● active | [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-spr256-frames/), [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/) |
| SPR256-PAL-003 | The leading 1024 bytes are a 256-entry palette of 4-byte entries; a 768-byte palette fits zero files. | High | ● active | [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-spr256-frames/) |
| SPR256-VAR-004 | The 6 projectile-arrow `.256` files carry no embedded palette, and their frames start at offset 0. | High | ● active | [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-spr256-frames/) |
| SPR256-EXC-005 | Eight `.256` sprites hold one frame followed by a trailing section that is not a frame record. | High / Unknown | ● active | [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-spr256-frames/), [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/) |
| SPR256-CORPUS-006 | Over the 1376 palette-bearing `.256` of this install there are 23 791 frames, and the maxima are width 640, height 480 and 256 frames per sprite. | High | ● active | [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-spr256-frames/) |

### SPR256-STRUCT-001

**Confidence.** High. The shipped loader reads the palette block and the
trailer: it gates the 1024-byte palette read on the trailer's bit 31
(`SPR256-TRLR-021`), and its constructor indexes exactly `frameCount` frames
(`SPR256-EXC-020`). The `w`-before-`h` order is a discriminated fact, not a
convention: `SPR256-RLE-008`'s row/column closure would fail on non-square
frames under the swap.

### SPR256-COUNT-002

- The count is walk-derived. The walk remains a valid independent derivation and
  agrees exactly with the count stored in the trailer's low `u16`.

**Confidence.** High. The loader computes `frameCount = trailer & 0x7FFFFFFF`
(`SPR256-TRLR-021`); the walk is the corroboration, not the proof.

**Amended.** Refined by `SPR256-TRLR-016`: the count is also stored, in the
trailer's low `u16`. The earlier clause that the count is "not stored anywhere"
is superseded.

### SPR256-PAL-003

- The palette is 256 entries × 4 bytes.

**Confidence.** High. The loader reads exactly 1024 bytes when the has-palette
bit is set (`SPR256-TRLR-021`). The 3-byte alternative is excluded both by that
length and by 0 files fitting it.

### SPR256-VAR-004

**Confidence.** High. `SPR256-TRLR-021` supplies the mechanism: the loader gates
the palette read on trailer bit 31, which is 0 in exactly these 6 files, so "no
palette, frames from 0" is what the shipped code does rather than what the bytes
allow. As a corpus observation alone the claim would be Medium.

### SPR256-EXC-005

**Confidence.** High that the 8 sprites hold one frame and a trailing non-record
section. Unknown for the section's structure.

**Unknown.** The structure of the trailing section. `SPR256-EXC-017`
characterises its bracketing and finds its inner block opaque.

**Amended.** Refined by `SPR256-EXC-017`: the section is bracketed
`0x80000001 … 0x80000001` (8/8).

### SPR256-CORPUS-006

- Bucket-B and the 6 no-palette arrows are excluded. `SPR256-RLE-019` counts the
  wider corpus at 23 895 frames; the two figures are not interchangeable.
- W=640 falsifies the carried-over hypothesis of a 512 width cap.
- The maxima feed RT-12.

**Confidence.** High. A census, not a model: the maxima are what the files
contain, and a single counterexample refutes the width-cap hypothesis.

## Run-length grammar

| ID | Claim | Confidence | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPR256-RLE-007 | Frame `data` is a run-length stream of `[2-bit op][6-bit count]` bytes: a literal run, transparent rows or transparent pixels, the cursor wrapping at width. | High | ● active | [EXP-0003](../experiments/EXP-0003-spr256-rle/) |
| SPR256-RLE-008 | Decoding is exact and lossless on 100 % of the 23 791 frames of the 1376 palette-bearing sprites. | High | ● active | [EXP-0003](../experiments/EXP-0003-spr256-rle/) |
| SPR256-RLE-009 | The `0b11` control quadrant (`0xC0–0xFF`) is never used in ROM1; observed counts are literal 0–63, skip ≤63 and blank-rows 1–63. | High | ● active | [EXP-0003](../experiments/EXP-0003-spr256-rle/) |
| SPR256-RLE-010 | The run-length grammar is corpus-universal: the frames of all 14 non-standard payloads decode by the same rules. | High | ● active | [EXP-0003](../experiments/EXP-0003-spr256-rle/) |

### SPR256-RLE-007

- `00` = literal-n: n index bytes follow.
- `01` = n transparent rows.
- `10` = n transparent px.
- The cursor wraps at width.

**Confidence.** High. The assignment is confirmed byte for byte against the
shipped decoder (`SPR256-RLE-022`). The corpus closure alone would be Medium,
and the fourth quadrant shows why: only the code says what `0xC0` means
(`SPR256-RLE-020`).

### SPR256-RLE-008

- Every row's tokens sum to exactly `width`, the run yields exactly `height`
  rows, and the stream is consumed fully.
- `Σ lit+skip+blankRows·w ≡ Σ w·h`.

**Confidence.** High. A measurement of exact closure, and the closure
discriminates in one direction: it fails under a swapped `w`/`h` or a shifted
opcode map. The grammar it closes over is the loader's own (`SPR256-RLE-022`).

### SPR256-RLE-009

**Confidence.** High. An absence measured exhaustively. `SPR256-RLE-020`
explains it: the quadrant is code-defined as an alias, so its absence in data is
a fact about the art, not about the format.

**Amended.** `SPR256-RLE-019`'s full-corpus count supersedes this measurement in
scope.

### SPR256-RLE-010

- A Bucket-B trailing section follows a cleanly decoded frame.

**Confidence.** High. Universality is a measurement over all 14; the grammar it
applies is the loader's (`SPR256-RLE-022`).

### SPR256-PAL-011

- The palette of `cursors/default.256` is the canonical IBM VGA palette, and
  matches it only under BGR.
- The 4th byte is 0 in 99.96 % of 350 720 entries (1370 palette-bearing sprites
  × 256). The 138 exceptions are two files (`SPR256-PAL-018`).

**Confidence.** High on the corpus alone. A known-answer test names and excludes
the competing RGB model: one shipped file's palette is the canonical IBM VGA
palette, which matches under BGR and does not match under RGB, so the two orders
are not both consistent with the bytes. This is the shape the confidence scale
allows. No `rom.exe` read corroborates it, so a loader that swapped channels on
upload would not have been caught.

### SPR256-PAL-012

- The `.256` blit path reaches transparency through the RLE opcodes, not through
  a colour-key test; nothing in `SPR256-RLE-020/022` tests for 0.
- The nearest instruction-level zero test is a different consumer:
  `FUN_0044c390`'s per-byte zero test on the terrain dirt overlay,
  `TERR-DIRT-017`.

**Confidence.** High for the absence: an exhaustive count over every literal
byte in the corpus, which cannot look different if true. Medium for the reading
that index 0 is the transparent key rather than an index the artists never used.

**Amended.** The Medium clause, "Palette index 0 = reserved
transparent/background key", is superseded by `SPR256-KEY-044` (EXP-0140;
[`retracted.md`](retracted.md)): the sprite pixel loops `FUN_0044db00` and
`FUN_00451ae0`, read whole, contain no compare and no branch on the source
value. The census and the word reserved stand; what falls is key.

### SPR256-PAL-013

- The `spritesb.256` "b"-variants are sparse overlay layers that borrow the
  sibling palette.
- `cursors/attack.256` and `pickup.256` decode to dense banded non-image
  content.

**Unknown.** The cursor-file anomaly is open.

### SPR256-OVL-014

- Pairing: 1 frame per base `sprites.256` frame, 180/180 match.
- Density 0.022 vs 0.266, ~12× sparser.
- Literal indices are zero-in-own-palette but coloured-in-base: 179/180 ≥99 %,
  median 100 %.
- Conclusion: the layer composites over the base using the base palette.
- Draw path (EXP-0053): the `b` sibling is the second sprite object at the table
  entry's `+0x08`. It is drawn by `vt+0x34` = `FUN_00428fc0`, which has the same
  six-argument lit signature as the base pass `vt+0x14`, so the overlay is
  level-shaded through the same table row as the base.
- Its two pixel loops, `FUN_0044dde0` (plain) and `FUN_0044eca0` (mirrored),
  read three operands where the base pass reads one: the source index through
  the shading row, the destination pixel (`0044dea0 MOV DX,word ptr [EDI]`) and
  the global `[0x005eb578]` (`0044ddee`). That is the blend.
- The pass is not object-only: the unit body pass issues it at `0045bcd5` and
  `0045bdff`.

**Confidence.** High for the measurements: the 180/180 frame pairing, the
density ratio, and the zero-in-own-palette/coloured-in-base test. Medium for the
conclusion "composites over base using base palette", an inference from those
three agreeing measurements. `AGENTS.md` carries the `spritesb` blend rule as an
open residual. Reading whichever `CSprite256` consumer resolves the `b` sibling
restores High.

**Unknown.** The blend arithmetic: the two overlay loops and their three
operands are located, but the blend itself is unread.

**Amended.** The Medium clause first rested on no `rom.exe` path read: nothing
had been shown to load the pair together or to blend them. EXP-0053 narrows it
to two pixel loops and confirms that the pair is drawn together (the draw path
above).

### SPR256-OVL-015

- Each has its own partial palette with the low 16 entries zero, and dense data.

**Unknown.** The cause.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPR256-TRLR-016 | The 4-byte trailer is `[u16 frameCount][u16 flag]` (LE): the low half equals the walk-derived frame count; the high half is `0x8000` with a palette, `0x0000` without. | High | ● active | [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/), [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/) |
| SPR256-EXC-017 | Each of the 8 Bucket-B files is a standard 1-frame sprite followed by an appended section bracketed `0x80000001 … 0x80000001` (8/8). | High | ● active | [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/), [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/) |
| SPR256-PAL-018 | The reserved 4th palette byte is non-zero only in `cursors/pickup.256` and `cursors/attack.256`: 138 of 350 720 standard entries. | High / Unknown | ● active | [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/) |
| SPR256-RLE-019 | The `0xC0-0xFF` (`0b11`) control quadrant occurs 0 times across the full `.256` corpus: 23 895 frames and 3 669 184 control bytes. | High | ● active | [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/), [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/) |

### SPR256-TRLR-016

- Agreement with the walk: standard 1370/1370, no-palette arrows 6/6, Bucket-B
  frame-terminator = 1.
- The low half `k` ranges over `[1..256]`, the frame-count domain; `k==count±1`
  holds on 0/1370.
- The high half is `0x8000` for palette-bearing sprites and `0x0000` for the
  no-palette arrows, whose trailer is a plain `u32` count.
- Bit 31 implies an impossible width, which makes the word the end-of-frames
  sentinel that stops a frame-walker.
- The frame count is therefore stored (refines COUNT-002), and the terminator is
  statically characterised.

**Confidence.** High for the count, and High for the high word as the
has-palette flag.

**Amended.** Refined by `SPR256-TRLR-021`: bit 31 is specifically the loader's
has-palette flag, and the count field is 31-bit.

### SPR256-EXC-017

- The standard part is a frame and the count-trailer `0x80000001`.
- The inner block (block − 8 marker bytes) is opaque. It is not a frame: its
  first `u32` has bit 31. It is not a same-width RLE image: 0/8 close at the
  frame width; a different width is not excluded. The large inners use bytes
  across the full `0x00-0xFF` range.
- Two size regimes: 4–16 B (small UI/projectile) and 303–2521 B dense (world-map
  tiles and the shop frame).
- A consistent sub-format, not junk. Refines EXC-005.
- `SPR256-EXC-020` resolves the runtime role: the shipped loader loads the
  section but never indexes or decodes it, so it is runtime-inert.

**Confidence.** High for the framing. The runtime role is resolved by
`SPR256-EXC-020`.

**Unknown.** The section's authoring origin; it is non-runtime.

### SPR256-PAL-018

- Non-zero counts: `cursors/pickup.256` 95, `cursors/attack.256` 43. All other
  1376 palette-bearing `.256` have an all-zero reserved column.
- The values scatter over `1..255` with no constant, at 106 distinct indices, so
  these two files' leading 1024 B is not a standard `[B,G,R,0]` palette.
- This ties the 4th-byte read to the OVL-015 anomaly.

**Confidence.** High for the measurement. Unknown for the meaning.

**Unknown.** What the non-zero reserved bytes mean.

### SPR256-RLE-019

- The population includes Bucket-B and the no-palette arrows.
- Control bytes: literal 1 341 762, blank-row 106 664, transparent 2 220 758.
- Extends RLE-009.
- `SPR256-RLE-020` resolves what the quadrant means: the loader aliases `0xC0`
  to `0x80` (a shared `else`; no 4th opcode). The absence measurement stands.

**Confidence.** High. An exhaustive count over every control byte in the corpus,
with the sub-totals printed so the total is checkable. Whether the loader
aliases `0xC0` to `0x80` or gives it a 4th meaning cannot be observed in the
data; the count leaves that question to `rom.exe` rather than guessing it.

## Shipped loader and decoder

| ID | Claim | Confidence | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPR256-RLE-020 | The `rom.exe` `.256` decoder dispatches three ways on `control & 0xC0`, and its `else` arm makes `0xC0` an alias of `0x80`, not a fourth opcode. | High | ● active | [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/) |
| SPR256-RLE-022 | The selected `rom.exe` draw decoder confirms the EXP-0003 opcode assignment byte for byte: `0x00` literal run, `0x40` skip rows, `0x80` transparent skip. | High | ● active (amended) | [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/), [EXP-0356](../experiments/EXP-0356-sprite-decoder-traces/) |
| SPR256-EXC-020 | The `CSprite256` constructor indexes exactly `frameCount` frames, so a Bucket-B file's appended block is loaded but never indexed, decoded or blitted. | High | ● active | [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/) |
| SPR256-TRLR-021 | The 4-byte trailer is `[31-bit frameCount][bit31 = has-palette]`: the loader masks out the count and gates the 1024-byte palette read on bit 31. | High | ● active | [EXP-0013](../experiments/EXP-0013-spr256-residual/), [EXP-0016](../experiments/EXP-0016-rom-spr256-decoder/) |

### SPR256-RLE-020

- Source: `rom.exe`. The decoder is a family of ~42 DirectDraw blit routines
  (`0x0044c660`‥`0x00452xxx`), of which 14 carry the decode. It is verified in
  full in `FUN_0x0044dd40` (stencil) and `FUN_0x0044dde0` (colour).
- Dispatch: `op = (CONCAT11(b,b) & 0xc03f) >> 8` and a three-way
  `if(0x00)/else if(0x40)/else`.
- The `else` handles both `0x80` and `0xC0` as a transparent skip of
  `control & 0x3F` px.
- `0xC0` is data-unused (RLE-019) but code-defined. This resolves the `rom.exe`
  flag on RLE-019.
- For family censuses: a raw function count over `0044c660..00453000` (66)
  includes funclets and is not the same measurement as "~42 blit routines".

**Confidence.** High. A three-way branch read in two routines. The fourth
quadrant's meaning is decidable only this way, since it never occurs in the
data. EXP-0040 re-checked the family on a repaired function table (the vtable
blind spot closed, 607 functions created) and confirmed it: the repair adds 3
functions inside `0044c660..00453000`, all 3–4 instruction MSVC SEH unwind
funclets carrying no RLE opcode constant, so no blit routine was hidden.

### SPR256-RLE-022

- Source: `rom.exe`.
- `0x00` = opaque run of `n` source palette-indices; `0x40` = skip `n` rows;
  `0x80` = transparent skip of `n` px; `n = control & 0x3F`.
- The row counter advances at signed column >= width without a clamp.
- `0x40` returns before destination advancement if remaining rows minus `n` is
  signed <= 0 (SPR256-062).
- Corroborates RLE-007…010 from the shipped decoder.

**Confidence.** High. The opcode assignment is read off the decoder, including
the row-end and frame-end conditions. This claim upgrades `SPR256-RLE-007` from
a grammar that fits to the grammar the game runs.

**Amended.** SPR256-062 (EXP-0356) amends the width/height shorthand: the row
threshold is signed>=, an oversized run is not clamped, partial columns survive
row controls, and row-control subtraction returns on <=0, including equality.
[`retracted.md`](retracted.md) holds the withdrawn shorthand: a row ends when
the column counter reaches width, and a row control ends the frame if its count
exceeds rows remaining.

### SPR256-EXC-020

- Source: `rom.exe`. The constructor is `FUN_0x00428c50`.
- For the 8 Bucket-B files `frameCount = 1`, from the final `0x80000001`
  trailer.
- The appended `0x80000001 … 0x80000001` inner block is read into the load
  buffer but never indexed, decoded, or blitted by any `CSprite256` method:
  runtime-inert.
- This resolves the `rom.exe` flag on EXC-017: what the loader does with the
  inner block is nothing.

**Confidence.** High. The loop bound is the trailer count and the class's
methods were enumerated, so "never indexed" is an absence over the whole
`CSprite256` surface, not an observation that it was not seen. EXP-0040
re-checked on a repaired function table (the vtable blind spot closed, 607
functions created) and confirmed it: the `CSprite256` vtable is `0x597418`,
installed at `00428c89`, with 44 slots and 35 distinct method targets, and every
one already had a function. The surface enumeration was never blind.

### SPR256-TRLR-021

- Source: `rom.exe`. The loader computes `frameCount = trailer & 0x7FFFFFFF` and
  gates the 1024-byte palette read on `trailer & 0x80000000`.
- This matches EXP-0013's split with no exception: 1370 palette sprites have
  bit31=1, and 6 no-palette arrows have bit31=0.
- Refines TRLR-016: the high half is a 1-bit palette flag, not a `0x8000` word,
  and the count field is 31-bit.
- Explains the no-palette variant (SPR256-VAR-004) as the same format with the
  flag off.

**Confidence.** High. Mask and gate are both quoted, and the claim explains a
previously separate observation instead of merely agreeing with it: the 1370/6
split falls out of the flag rather than being fitted to it.

### SPR256-FRAME-023

- Population: every `.256` node in the install, 1384 parsed and 8 unparsable.
  1253 carry a single `(w,h)` across all frames and 131 do not; one sheet holds
  up to 5 distinct sizes.
- Two object sheets have load-bearing per-frame differences:
  `objects/stones/sprites.256` (9 frames, `64×80` and `64×64`) and
  `objects/pointer/sprites.256` (6 frames, `16×32` and `24×24`). Nine and six
  `objects.reg` classes respectively select different frames of them by `Index`
  (`TERR-SPR-042`).
- This is what makes the engine's own two anchor computations disagree: the body
  pass measures the drawn frame, the shadow pass measures frame 0
  (`TERR-SPR-043`).
- It makes `TERR-SPR-040`'s "a frame of a different size is centred inside the
  `Width × Height` canvas first" a live case rather than a hypothetical.

**Confidence.** High. A measurement over every `.256` in the install with no
model interposed: the frame record's `[w][h][size]` header is
`SPR256-STRUCT-001`, and the count is `SPR256-TRLR-021`'s trailer.

### SPR256-UNIT-024

- `FUN_0045bf00` (CUnit `vt+0x2c`, `TERR-SPR-048`) selects a frame with two
  hard-coded constant pairs chosen by the class's `Flip`: `S = 16, D = 8` when
  `Flip == 0`, `S = 9, D = 5` otherwise (`REG-UNITS-051`). `S` is the standing
  block's length and `D` the number of directions the sheet stores.
- With `MB, MV, AT, DY, BN, ID` for
  `MoveBeginPhases, MovePhases, AttackPhases, DyingPhases, BonePhases, IdlePhases`,
  the blocks tile the sheet in this order and at these absolute bases:
  - `0` stand, `S` frames; the index is the facing itself.
  - `S` move, `D×(MB+MV)` frames. Move-begin occupies the first `MB` of each
    direction's slot and the move loop the remaining `MV`, since the arm adds
    `MoveBeginPhases` (`0045c1ae`/`0045c1cb`).
  - `S + D·(MB+MV)` attack, `D×AT`.
  - `S + D·(MB+MV+AT)` dying, `D×DY`.
  - `S + D·(MB+MV+AT+DY)` bone, `D×BN`, and idle, `D×ID`, at the same base. The
    two never collide because no shipped class carries both (0 of 34).
- Within a block the index is `dir × <that block's phase count> + k`, with `k`

### SPR256-STR-041

- Source: `rom.exe`. The two tests are in `FUN_0045a0a0`, `0045a172`…`0045a1da`.
- Base `[0, TW·FH)`: frame = the cell index.
- Animation `[TW·FH, TW·FH + maxAnimFrame·live)`: frame =
  `TW·FH + (phaseFrame−1)·live + rank(cell)`. `phaseFrame` is the class
  timeline's current value, `live` = `AnimMask`'s non-`'-'` count, and `rank`
  counts live cells before this one; a `'-'` cell falls back to its base frame.
- Ruin `[frames − TW·FH, frames)`: frame = `frames − TW·FH + cellIndex`, taken
  when `health <= 0` and `Indestructible == 0`. The ruin block is addressed from
  the end of the file, not from a stored base.
- Corpus, re-executing the loader's own `AnimTime`/`AnimFrame` expansion:
  `frames == k·TW·FH + maxAnimFrame·live` with `k ∈ {1,2}` on 64 of 66 classes
  (`k = 2` on 39, `k = 1` on 25).
- The two exceptions are `bridge1v` (9 frames) and `bridge2` (14). Their
  drawable is a C++ subclass with its own selector (`TERR-STRUCT-105`), and
  their `1×1` grid is meaningless.
- Where `k = 1` no ruin art ships and `frames − TW·FH` is 0, so a destroyed such
  structure re-draws its intact art rather than reading out of range.
- `Indestructible` gates the draw, not the sheet: 4 of the 39 `k = 2` classes
  are `Indestructible` and ship an unreachable ruin grid.

**Confidence.** High. All three bases and both gates are named instructions. The
identity re-executes over the shipped registry and sheets at 64/66, with the two
misses predicted in advance by the reading, and the `-mode art` montages show
the base grid over the ruin grid as one coherent picture per class. A failure
would have looked like a residual on a class the reading does not exempt, or a
montage whose two halves are not the same building intact and wrecked.

### SPR256-KEY-044

- Source: `rom.exe`.
- `FUN_0044db00`, the plain `.256` pixel loop, decodes the control byte as
  `AL; AH = AL; EAX &= 0xc03f` and dispatches on `AH` being 0 / 0x40 / else. On
  the literal arm it does `0044dbf8 MOV AX,word ptr [EBX + EAX*0x2]` followed by
  an unconditional `MOV dword ptr [EDI + -0x4],EDX`: no `CMP`, no `TEST`, no
  branch on the byte anywhere between the fetch and the store.
- `FUN_00451ae0`, the `.16a` loop, has the same shape: its only tests are
  `TEST AX,0x4000` and `TEST AX,0x8000`, on the control word.
- Transparency in both formats is therefore entirely the RLE's skip and
  blank-rows opcodes, and for `.16a` also the per-pixel alpha of
  `SPR16A-ALPHA-025`. That alpha is a continuum, so a binary mask cannot express
  a `.16a` sheet at all.
- `SPR256-PAL-012`'s census stands: index 0 never occurs as a literal in the
  1 376 palette-bearing `.256`, and is reserved in the sense that no artist
  emitted it. Its Medium reading that 0 is "the transparent key" does not: no
  sprite blit reaches transparency by testing it.
- `FUN_0044c390`'s per-byte zero test, the nearest instruction-level support
  `SPR256-PAL-012` could point at, is a different consumer (the terrain dirt
  overlay, `TERR-DIRT-017`) and remains that.
- G2: a consumer must not implement a colour-key test for these two formats.
  Doing so changes nothing on shipped art, because index 0 never occurs, and
  diverges immediately on any art a customisation adds.

**Confidence.** High. Two pixel loops read instruction by instruction over their
whole bodies. The claim is an absence inside a bounded routine, the one shape in
which an absence can be read directly rather than swept for.
